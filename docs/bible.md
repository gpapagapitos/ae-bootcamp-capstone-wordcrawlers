# Wordcrawlers Bible

## Purpose

Single source for world lore, tone, factions, enemy fantasy, and naming conventions.

Implementation guardrail for new content proposals: docs/content-canon-checklist.md

## Tone Pillars

- Reclaimed-earth fantasy: nature has reclaimed the surface after the vaults opened.
- Words are literal weapons, but the world feels botanical, ancient, and rebuilt rather than neon or noir.
- The tone should feel adventurous, weathered, hopeful, and dangerous.

## Canon Rules

1. Consistency over novelty: new lore must not contradict established canon.
2. Mechanics and lore should reinforce each other.
3. Card names must communicate gameplay role quickly.

## Factions

### The Binding Order

Caretakers of the Lexicon Vault; believe old language must be cataloged before the wild surface can be trusted.

### The Inkbound

Rogue spellwrights who weaponize unstable words and overgrown relics.

### The Quiet Press

Silent archivists preserving forbidden chapter-fragments and seed-memory records.

## Letters as Cards

Letters are the smallest playable artifacts in the game. They should feel like field tools, relic fragments, and living glyphs rather than abstract tiles.

### Core Letter Families

- Striking letters: hard consonants that push damage, pressure, or tempo.
- Flow letters: vowels that smooth composition, draw, or combo extension.
- Root letters: stable letters that support long words, sustain, and repair.
- Wild letters: flexible glyphs that adapt to many words but carry a cost or limit.
- Relic letters: rare letters with a signature effect tied to the world fantasy.

### Card Roles

1. Builder cards

- Basic letters used to form words and satisfy strict dictionary rules.
- Flavor: field stones, bark shards, seed-stamps, scratched tags.

2. Tactic cards

- Modify how letters behave this turn or next turn.
- Examples: draw, lock, recycle, refine, sharpen, preserve.

3. Relic cards

- Persistent or high-impact effects that define a run.
- Examples: bonus damage for repeated roots, extra draw after long words, hex on rare letters.

4. Reaction cards

- Trigger when a word is submitted, when damage is dealt, or when a turn resolves.
- Examples: refund energy, convert block into damage, cleanse fatigue, echo a letter.

### Effect Vocabulary

- Draw: add more letters to hand.
- Lock: keep a letter from being discarded.
- Refine: replace a letter or upgrade its value.
- Preserve: keep a composed letter or word bonus for later.
- Echo: repeat part of a word effect.
- Root: grant stability, block, or sustain.
- Hex: add enemy counters that fuel special effects.
- Stun: interrupt enemy action.

### Rarity Tiers

- Common: dependable building letters and low-complexity tactics.
- Uncommon: stronger synergies, conditional bonuses, and tighter identity.
- Rare: high-impact run-shaping letters or relics with clear tradeoffs.
- Boss/legendary: unique identity cards tied to major enemies, heroes, or vault myths.

### Canon Examples

- Mossmarked Vowel: common flow letter that helps long-word construction and mild sustain.
- Rootbound Consonant: common striking letter that adds attack stability.
- Brass Sigil: uncommon relic letter that rewards precise word length.
- Seed Token: tactic card that draws and preserves a chosen letter.
- Vault Echo: rare reaction card that repeats the last successful letter effect once per fight.

## Enemy Naming Pattern

- Standard: adjective + role (e.g., Hollow Scrivener)
- Elite: epithet + title (e.g., Gilded Censor)
- Boss: proper title phrase (e.g., The Ink Warden)
